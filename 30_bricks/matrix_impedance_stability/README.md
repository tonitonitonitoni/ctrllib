# Constant matrix impedance stability

Reader routes: [interface](interface.md) · [conventional proof](proof.md) · [references](correspondence/references.md) · [exact statement appendix](correspondence/statements.md).

This is a Generated Brick: an agent-proposed project derivation; the label

alongside the [captured endpoint statements](correspondence/statements.md).

## Notation

Let $z=(v,e)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$. The unforced equation is $M\dot v+Dv+Ke=0$, $\dot e=v$, with energy $V=\frac12v^TMv+\frac12e^TKe$.

## Statement

The explicit global flow $\Phi(t,z)=\exp(tA)z$, where $A(v,e)=(-M^{-1}(Dv+Ke),v)$, solves the equation. Its origin is Lyapunov stable and every initial state converges to zero as $t\to+\infty$.

## Hypotheses

The dimension is finite and nonempty. The constant real matrices $M,D,K$ are symmetric positive definite. Flow existence, solution identity and forward precompactness are proved; they are not caller assumptions.

## Proof

The exponential of a bounded linear operator supplies continuity, the group law and the derivative. Symmetry and the inertia inverse give $\dot V=-v^TDv\le0$. A positive quadratic lower bound gives strict positivity off zero and precompactness of forward orbits. Lyapunov's stability theorem supplies stability. On an invariant zero-dissipation trajectory, positive damping gives $v=0$, and then the equation and invertible stiffness give $e=0$. The existing CoM LaSalle theorem supplies global convergence.

## What the result establishes and does not

It establishes stability and convergence of the specified ideal matrix equation, including its global flow. It does not identify that equation with a physical rover or a variable-gain controller.

## Limitations

This is an ideal Euclidean task equation. It does not cover time-varying impedance, contact inputs, flexible modes, actuator limits, sampled control, or a global orientation representation. A physical robot still needs the exact plant/controller/coordinate correspondence.
